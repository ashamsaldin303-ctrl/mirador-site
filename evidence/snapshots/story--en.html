Story

CHAPTER ONE

The Climb

There is no valet. There is a lift that takes its time, and a final flight of stairs where the noise of the street drops away with every step. By the last landing, the city is already below you — a field of small lights going about its evening. We chose the sixth floor for exactly this: the moment the doors open and the street turns into a view.

CHAPTER TWO

The Room

One room, forty seats, a single long window facing west. Materials are few and honest: darkened oak, brushed brass, linen. Light comes from low amber sources, as if the room itself were lit by the city below. Nothing on the walls — the skyline is the art.

CHAPTER THREE

The Evening

Service begins at six and ends when it ends. Courses leave the pass in their own order; the fire decides some of it. Ask for the ribeye for two and you will see why it needs the whole evening.

We kept only what the night required.
